What happened

A syndicate pasted members' faces onto photos from lost Hong Kong ID cards to pass remote onboarding at seven banks, opening 19 accounts that laundered about HK$190 million; 22 people were arrested.

Impact: Accounts linked to at least 14 local scams with losses of about HK$1.68 million, roughly US$216,000, and about HK$190 million in suspected criminal proceeds moved in a year.

Who triggered it

Actor: Hong Kong triad-linked fraud syndicate

Attribution: Hong Kong Island Regional Crime Unit identified two ringleaders, five core members and 15 mule account holders, some with triad backgrounds.

Motive: Obtain loans and credit cards and launder proceeds of phone, investment and shopping scams.

How it happened

Attack vector: AI face-swapping of lost ID card images used in 23 online account applications between September 2024 and April 2025; 19 passed KYC checks.

Root cause: Remote onboarding relied on selfie-to-ID matching that could be defeated by synthetic faces built on genuine stolen documents.

Timeline

  1. Sep 1, 2024Syndicate begins submitting AI-altered account applications
  2. Oct 8, 2025Police make arrests across Hong Kong over two days
  3. Oct 10, 2025Police announce 22 arrests and HK$190 million in laundered funds

Response

Arrests for conspiracy to defraud and money laundering; phones, laptops and printers seized; regulators and banks told to strengthen remote identity checks.

Current status: Under investigation
