Case studies

Selected cases, with what happened and how schools and authorities responded, as recorded in each case file.

Deepfake abuseSouth KoreaAug 28, 2024

South Korea school deepfake crisis: Telegram rooms target students; President Yoon orders crackdown

What happened
In August 2024 Telegram chatrooms tied to schools and universities were found sharing AI sexual deepfakes of female students, including one with about 1,200 members targeting more than 30 Inha University students; police said 73.6% of suspects identified in early 2024 were minors. President Yoon ordered a crackdown, police began a seven-month special operation, and on 26 September parliament made possessing or watching sexual deepfakes a crime.
How schools and authorities responded
Seven-month KNPA crackdown with detection software; National Assembly criminalised buying, saving or watching sexual deepfakes (up to 3 years) and raised the maximum for making them to 7 years
Status
Ongoing

Deepfake abuseSpainSep 19, 2023

AI 'undress' app used to make fake nudes of 20+ schoolgirls in Almendralejo; 15 minors later convicted

What happened
Boys aged 12 to 14 took girls' photos from social media, used an AI app to put their faces on naked bodies and shared the images in WhatsApp and Telegram groups, prompting at least 22 police complaints. In July 2024 the Badajoz juvenile court convicted 15 minors on 20 counts of creating child sexual abuse material and 20 counts against moral integrity, giving each a year of probation.
How schools and authorities responded
National Police and the Central Cybercrime Unit investigated, the case went to the juvenile prosecutor and Spain's data protection agency opened its own inquiry; 15 boys got one year of probation plus classes on gender equality and responsible technology use
Status
Resolved

Deepfake abuseUnited StatesJan 17, 2024

Athletic director frames Pikesville High principal with AI-generated racist and antisemitic audio

What happened
An audio clip appearing to show Pikesville High principal Eric Eiswert making racist and antisemitic remarks spread on social media in January 2024; police and an FBI-contracted analyst found it was AI-generated with human editing. Former athletic director Dazhon Darien was arrested in April 2024 and in April 2025 took an Alford plea to disturbing school operations, receiving four months in jail.
How schools and authorities responded
Arrest on 25 April 2024 on charges including stalking, theft, retaliation against a witness and disrupting school operations; Alford plea in April 2025 with other charges dropped; Eiswert sued the school district and Darien in January 2025.
Status
Resolved

AI misfireUnited StatesOct 25, 2025

Omnilert AI gun detection flags a student's chip bag at a Baltimore County school; police handcuff him

What happened
Omnilert's AI camera system flagged an empty Doritos bag held by a student after football practice as a possible firearm, and armed police searched and handcuffed him. The district's security team had already cancelled the alert, but the principal, unaware of the cancellation, reported it to the school resource officer, who called police.
How schools and authorities responded
Omnilert expressed regret but said the process functioned as intended; county council members called for a review of the system; the superintendent said reviews are part of regular practice
Status
Resolved

Chatbot harmLebanonOct 14, 2026

Lebanese family sues OpenAI, alleging ChatGPT contributed to their 15-year-old son's suicide

What happened
The parents of Matias Habib, who died aged 15 in April 2026, filed a wrongful-death suit against OpenAI and Sam Altman in San Francisco, alleging ChatGPT steered him towards nihilism and failed to act when he asked about suicide.
How schools and authorities responded
Suit filed in the Superior Court of California, County of San Francisco, by the Social Media Victims Law Center and the Tech Justice Law Project, within the coordinated ChatGPT proceeding (JCCP). OpenAI had not commented publicly at the time of reporting.
Status
Ongoing

AI exam fraudGHDec 18, 2023

WAEC withholds WASSCE results for candidates at 235 Ghanaian schools over AI-generated answers

What happened
The West African Examinations Council withheld subject results of candidates from 235 schools in Ghana for collusion and use of AI-generated answers in the 2023 WASSCE. WAEC said some candidates copied chatbot error replies such as "I'm not familiar with the term you have used" into their answer booklets.
How schools and authorities responded
Results withheld pending investigation; WAEC said school staff and parent complicity could not be ruled out
Status
Under investigation

Model & data leakUnited StatesMar 12, 2025

AP/Seattle Times obtain ~3,500 unredacted student files flagged by Gaggle AI monitoring in Vancouver, WA

What happened
A records request by The Seattle Times and AP returned links to nearly 3,500 unredacted screenshots of student writing flagged by Gaggle's AI surveillance, covering suicide, depression, abuse and sexuality, viewable by anyone with the link and without a password. At least six students were potentially outed to school officials over writing about being gay or transgender.
How schools and authorities responded
District apologised; Gaggle changed the system so screenshots require login after 72 hours

Prevention measures in place

Measures that ministries, regulators, exam bodies and international organisations have adopted or published, with what each one does, as stated in the document.

Ministries and governments

  • UK Department for Education · England · 2025Generative artificial intelligence (AI) in educationDfE policy (updated August 2025) says safety should be the top priority, pupils should use GenAI only with safeguards such as supervision and filtering and monitoring, and personal data should not be put into GenAI tools. It warns GenAI can make cyber attacks and impersonation emails more credible and points to JCQ and Ofqual rules on AI malpractice.
  • UK Department for Education · England · 2026Generative AI: product safety standardsStandards for edtech suppliers (first issued January 2025, expanded 19 January 2026) covering filtering, monitoring and reporting to the safeguarding lead, security against jailbreaking, privacy, testing and governance. The 2026 update adds cognitive development, emotional and social development, mental health and anti-manipulation standards, including avoiding anthropomorphism and emotional dependence and detecting distress.
  • UK Department for Education · England · 2026Cyber security standards for schools and collegesSets standards including annual cyber risk assessment, staff and student training, MFA for staff cloud/remote and admin accounts, patching critical vulnerabilities within 14 days, and a 3-2-1 style backup plan. It also requires reporting cyber attacks internally and to bodies such as insurers and the DfE (last updated September 2026).
  • UK Council for Internet Safety / DfE · England · 2024Sharing nudes and semi-nudes: advice for education settings working with children and young peopleThe March 2024 update explicitly covers AI-generated and digitally manipulated 'pseudo-images' of under-18s and says the law applies to children who make them. It tells staff to refer to the designated safeguarding lead, avoid viewing, copying or sharing images, refer to police or social care where needed, and use removal tools such as IWF/Childline Report Remove.
  • UK Department for Education · England · 2026Keeping children safe in education 2026Statutory safeguarding guidance for schools and colleges, with the 2026 version in force from 1 September 2026. DfE's GenAI guidance names it as the core reference for online safety, filtering and monitoring and cyber security when schools use AI.
  • New York State Education Department · New York State, USA · 2023Determination on biometric identifying technology in schoolsThe September 2023 determination bars New York schools from buying or using facial recognition technology, after a state report found the risks outweighed benefits, including higher misidentification rates for children and other groups. Other biometric tools are left to local decisions that must weigh privacy, civil rights, effectiveness and parental input.
  • Republic of Korea Ministry of Education (reported by The Korea Herald) · South Korea · 2024Education Ministry response to school deepfake sex crimesIn August 2024 the ministry set up an emergency task force to track school deepfake cases weekly, handle victim reports, give psychological support and strengthen digital-ethics education. It said high-school perpetrators could face expulsion, the top sanction in the school-violence system.
  • Republic of Korea Ministry of Education (reported by The Korea Times) · South Korea · 2024799 students, 31 teachers victimized by deepfake videos this yearMinistry survey data showed 833 school victims of deepfakes in 2024 to late October, including 799 students and 31 teachers. Schools referred 417 cases for investigation and 218 for deletion of videos.
  • Ireland Department of Education and Youth · Ireland · 2025Guidance on Artificial Intelligence in SchoolsNational guidance published 21 October 2025 with Oide to help schools plan safe, ethical and appropriate AI use, drawing on UNESCO and EU work. It notes the State Examinations Commission requires AI-generated material to be referenced, and a taskforce on AI in education including assessment was announced.
  • UAE Ministry of Education (reported by Education Middle East) · United Arab Emirates · 2026Safe and Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in Classrooms (manual)The 2026 manual bans generative AI for students under 13 or below Year 7, bans AI in formal exams and undisclosed AI work, and prohibits entering personal data of students, teachers or parents into AI tools. It also prohibits deepfakes and impersonation and requires direct teacher oversight of classroom AI use.
  • Saudi Ministry of Education and SDAIA (reported by Gulf News) · Saudi Arabia · 2025Guidelines for using generative AI in educationJoint guidebook for students, teachers and parents aimed at ethical and responsible use that keeps the teacher central. It says students under 13 should not use GenAI without direct supervision and under-18s need clear parental consent.
  • Center for Educational Research and Development (CRDP), Lebanon · Lebanon · 2025Lebanon's AI Competency Framework / AI Educational Framework for students and teachersCRDP, which develops Lebanon's national curriculum, published (October 2025) an AI framework whose first part is an AI policy for Lebanese schools built on UNESCO's 2024 frameworks and 2021 AI ethics recommendation. Its guiding principles include a human-centred approach, data privacy and security, inclusivity and teacher professional development.
  • Egypt Ministry of Education and Technical Education (reported by Egyptian Streets) · Egypt · 2025AI curricula in schools from 2025-2026The minister announced AI curricula in selected primary, preparatory and secondary grades from 2025-26, including the ethics and social implications of AI. No published usage or safeguarding guideline from the ministry was found.

Regulators and law enforcement

  • European Union · European Union · 2024Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act), Annex III point 3 (education)Classifies as high-risk AI used to decide admission or assignment to education, to evaluate learning outcomes, to assess the level of education a person will receive, and to monitor and detect prohibited behaviour by students during tests. High-risk systems carry obligations such as risk management, human oversight and documentation.
  • UK Information Commissioner's Office · United Kingdom · 2024Essex school reprimanded after using facial recognition technology for canteen paymentsThe ICO reprimanded Chelmer Valley High School (23 July 2024) for introducing facial recognition without a data protection impact assessment, without consulting its DPO, parents or pupils, and relying on opt-out consent. It stresses a DPIA is required before biometric systems and that explicit opt-in consent is needed.
  • US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) · United States (K-12) · 2023Protecting Our Future: Partnering to Safeguard K-12 Organizations from Cybersecurity ThreatsCISA's January 2023 report tells schools to start with the most impactful measures: MFA, patching known exploited vulnerabilities, tested backups, an exercised incident response plan and ongoing training. It also urges pressing vendors for secure-by-default products and joining information-sharing groups such as MS-ISAC and K12 SIX.
  • US Federal Bureau of Investigation (IC3) · United States · 2025Senior US Officials Impersonated in Malicious Messaging Campaign (PSA I-051525-PSA)Warns that criminals are using AI-generated voice messages and texts to impersonate senior officials and steal account access. It advises verifying identity through a known number, not clicking links until verified, enabling two-factor authentication, using agreed code words, and confirming payment requests through a separate channel.
  • US Federal Trade Commission · United States · 2025FTC launches inquiry into AI chatbots acting as companionsOn 11 September 2025 the FTC ordered seven firms, including Alphabet, Character.AI, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI, to explain how they test, monitor and limit harms of companion chatbots to children. It asks about monetising engagement, age-restriction enforcement, disclosures to parents and use of conversation data.
  • eSafety Commissioner (Australia) · Australia · 2026Toolkit for Schools: Guide to responding to image-based abuse involving AI deepfakesStep-by-step guide (first released mid-2025, current PDF dated March 2026) for school leaders responding to nude or sexual deepfakes of students or staff, designed to sit alongside child-protection policies. eSafety urges schools to prioritise the wellbeing of targeted students and staff, report possible crimes to police, meet mandatory reporting duties and use eSafety for rapid removal.
  • eSafety Commissioner (Australia) · Australia · 2025Online safety advisory: AI chatbots and companions - risks to children and young peopleThe 18 February 2025 advisory warns that AI companions simulate personal relationships, allow unregulated discussion of sex, self-harm and suicide, and often lack effective age checks. eSafety later issued legal notices (October 2025) to Character.AI, Nomi, Chai and Chub AI and reported that most companions failed to refer users to support for suicide or self-harm.

Exam and quality bodies

  • Joint Council for Qualifications (JCQ) · UK (GCSE, A level and other general qualifications) · 2025AI Use in Assessments: Your role in protecting the integrity of qualificationsGuidance effective 30 April 2025 treats AI misuse that leaves work not the student's own as malpractice and requires centres' malpractice policies to cover AI. It recommends supervised in-class work, checking drafts, short viva-style discussions and restricting AI on centre devices, says detectors must not be relied on alone, and says AI cannot be the sole marker.
  • Quality Assurance Agency for Higher Education (QAA) · UK higher education · 2023Maintaining quality and standards in the ChatGPT era: advice on generative AI toolsQAA advises universities that managed use of generative AI is preferable to outright bans, while maintaining academic standards. It offers a four-step process for identifying assessments that need adjusting and urges robust internal quality assurance.
  • Tertiary Education Quality and Standards Agency (TEQSA) · Australia (higher education) · 2025Enacting assessment reform in a time of artificial intelligenceNon-prescriptive TEQSA resource (released September 2025) that helps universities manage generative AI risks to the assurance of learning while teaching ethical use. It maps the main strategic pathways institutions are taking to redesign assessment.

International organisations

  • UNESCO · Global · 2023Guidance for generative AI in education and researchUNESCO's first global guidance on generative AI in education (published September 2023, page updated January 2026) asks governments to regulate GenAI tools and build coherent policy frameworks for education and research. It proposes an age limit for independent conversations with GenAI platforms, mandatory data-privacy protection, and an ethical validation process for tools because most schools cannot yet vet them.
  • UNESCO · Global · 2024AI competency framework for studentsSets out 12 competencies across four dimensions (human-centred mindset, ethics of AI, AI techniques and applications, AI system design) at three levels: understand, apply, create. It is meant to help curricula prepare students to engage with AI safely and to exercise critical judgement of AI solutions.
  • UNESCO · Global · 2024AI competency framework for teachersDefines 15 competencies across five dimensions, including ethics of AI, AI pedagogy and AI for professional learning, at three levels: acquire, deepen, create. It is built on protecting teachers' rights and enhancing human agency, and gives strategies for putting ethical principles into practice.
  • UNICEF · Global · 2025Guidance on AI and children, version 3.0The December 2025 update of UNICEF's policy guidance sets ten requirements for child-centred AI, including regulatory oversight, child safety, protection of children's data and privacy, non-discrimination, transparency and accountability, and building children's AI skills. It comes with a checklist for governments and companies.
  • UNICEF · Global · 2026'Deepfake abuse is abuse' (statement)UNICEF's 4 February 2026 statement cites UNICEF/ECPAT/INTERPOL research finding at least 1.2 million children in 11 countries had their images turned into sexual deepfakes in the past year. It calls on governments to bring AI-generated content into child sexual abuse material laws and criminalise its creation, acquisition, possession and sharing, and on AI firms to build in safety by design and prevent circulation.
  • OECD · OECD members · 2026OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026: Exploring effective uses of generative AI in educationFinds that general-purpose GenAI boosts task performance without real learning gains unless guided by pedagogy, and that students' advantage can disappear or reverse in exams once AI access is removed. It recommends purpose-built educational tools, equitable infrastructure and sustained professional learning.
  • Council of Europe · 46 Council of Europe member states · 2026Committee of Ministers recommendation on AI literacyRecommendation to member states to help pupils, students, teachers, parents and citizens understand AI, use it responsibly and recognise where it threatens human rights, democracy and the rule of law. It gives effect to Article 20 of the Council of Europe Framework Convention on AI.
  • European Commission · European Union · 2026Ethical guidelines on the use of AI and data in teaching and learning for educators (updated)First issued in 2022 and updated in 2026 through the European Digital Education Hub, the guidelines explain the GDPR and AI Act context for teachers. They provide guiding questions and case examples for judging AI tools before using them in class.
