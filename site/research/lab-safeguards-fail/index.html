AI developers' own safety measures fail

Seen in real incidents12 matching case files

ShareLinkedInXFacebookWhatsAppTelegramEmail

The warning

Organisations building advanced AI may lack a strong safety culture, so tests, controls and warnings fail when they matter.

Paraphrased from the paper. Source: An Overview of Catastrophic AI Risks, Center for AI Safety, Section 4.2, page 29.

What the registry shows

Several AI developers and a government institute ran tests whose agents got out and reached real companies and government systems; some victims learned of it before the developer did.

Verified case files show this risk happening in the real world. First real-world case on file: Sep 18, 2023.

Research vs reality →

How we match: a case file is linked to a warning when what happened is a real-world instance of the risk the paper describes, judged by the editors from the facts in the case file. A match means the case fits the warning, not that it proves the paper's wider argument. Allegations in lawsuits are labelled as allegations. Statuses are editorial and reviewed as new cases arrive. The matching is recomputed on every update. Data: Systematic coverage in the registry starts in 2025, so a first-case date shows when the registry first recorded the risk, not when it first happened. research.json (CC BY 4.0)
